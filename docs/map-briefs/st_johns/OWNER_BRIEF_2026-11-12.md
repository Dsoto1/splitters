# St. John's: the owner's brief for a rework (2026-11-12)

The owner knows the real campus. This is what they said and what their twenty-one reference photos and the campus plan show. Nothing is built
from it yet; more references are coming ("I'll add reference more after this"). The photos are not in the repository
(they are the school's own pictures, not ours to ship or commit): ask the owner for them, or look in the session
scratchpad `stjohns-refs/` while that session lives. Use them as reference only: nothing from them goes into a texture.

## What the owner said

- "St. John's map seems to use old custom stairs." (Replace them with the current stair pieces.)
- "There are many buildings with wide open walls. The windows could definitely be improved."
- The barracks / dorms: "each floor would have a long hallway down the middle, with rooms on either side (rooms had a
  desk and shelves for 2 cadets, and a bunk bed. Each room had a window, a sink, and 2 small/slim closets to left and
  right behind when walking in the room. Each room has a big thick wooden door with cast iron hardware."
- "The main entry way to campus is not a skywalk, that is seen elsewhere in real campus."
- The school building: "multi level building with hall down middle and classrooms on either side."
- References also given for the admin building's inside, the mess hall, and "some of the grounds in the center
  between library and chapel".

## What the photos show (ten, described so the build does not depend on the files)

1. **Dorm room, two views.** A small room: a sink in a wood vanity just inside, a wood desk with a hutch and a
   chair, a tall single sash window with a deep sill, a wood bunk bed (dark bedding) against the side wall, speckled
   grey floor, plain painted walls.
2. **Gym, entrance end.** A brick end wall about one storey high under a barrel-vault roof on steel trusses, a
   double-door opening in its middle leading to a lit corridor, a red banner over the door, sports banners along the
   wall top, a balcony rail above the brick, a maple court floor.
3. **Gym, the court.** A full basketball court under the same long arched truss roof, rows of hanging lights, glass
   block windows down both long walls, a flag on the far wall, bleacher-height brick side walls.
4. **The chapel from the grounds.** A long grey limestone building with a row of tall arched windows between
   buttresses, a crenellated parapet, low shrubs along its foot, a lawn with mature trees in front, a dark stone
   class marker in the grass.
5. **A stone building's side path.** Rough coursed limestone walls, arched and square windows with stone surrounds,
   a concrete path curving along the wall, shrubs and evergreens.
6. **A classroom in use.** Pennants round the top of the walls, a chalkboard and a screen on the front wall, loose
   desks and tables, wood trim.
7. and 8. **Classrooms, empty (the same pair twice).** A bright room, rows of single student desks with blue chairs,
   fluorescent strip lights, a wall of large sash windows on one side, a teacher's desk and screen at the front, a
   polished tan floor.
9. **A stone monument on the grounds.** A tall fieldstone cairn with an arched niche near its top, on a stone base in
   a mulched island where paths meet; a square crenellated limestone tower with a round window behind it.
10. **The admin building inside.** Dark wood wainscot and door frames, a heavy panelled wood door with a diamond
    pattern, a half-glazed office door, framed pictures, a wood plank ceiling, an office seen through interior windows.

### Second set (the same day): four more photos and two more notes

The owner: "For dorm floors, there is also an office in middle on right side, with bathrooms and middle stairway
directly across from it. Also noticed one of the buildings is partly 2 floors then 3 floors, it should just be 3
floors."

11. **The chapel inside.** A tall nave of warm coursed limestone, a great round arch before the chancel with a round
    stained-glass rose window high in the end wall, a green curtain behind the altar, tall narrow stained-glass
    windows down both side walls, rows of dark wood pews either side of a centre aisle, national flags hanging from
    the dark timber roof and standing by the chancel, wall sconces, hymn boards.
12. **The mess hall.** A long hall under a dark wood beam-and-plank ceiling, a row of square limestone piers down
    one side with round-arched windows beyond, long tables with black chairs in rows, flags hung along the piers,
    round iron chandeliers, a fireplace wall at the far end.
13. **The chapel from the lawn** (wider than photo 4): the row of tall arched windows over a low arcade of smaller
    openings, a small cannon on the lawn before it, a flagpole, the class marker stone.
14. **The centre of the grounds.** A brick path through lawn to a low stone building with a tall red conical roof
    over a round-arched entrance, stone benches on mulched beds, black lamp posts with banners, bare mature trees,
    a crenellated limestone hall behind.

So a dorm floor is: a hall down the middle, rooms both sides, and at its middle an office on the right with the
bathrooms and the middle stairway across the hall from it. And the building that is two floors for part of its
length is three floors throughout.

### Third set (the same day): two more photos, the structure as a whole

The owner: "You can see the full structure behind the trees. We have most of this built, they are just not put
together in this way. You can also see what this building (the one that is half 2 floors half 3) is actually looking
like. It had a larger center (on each floor there is a door to a TAC officer's family living space by the middle
stairwell). They should be half-turn stairwells on either ends, but a straight run for the middle ones."

15. **The clock tower and the arch.** A three-storey limestone hall with rows of square windows trimmed in red
    stone, a square crenellated clock tower rising from its corner (a round clock face under paired arched belfry
    openings), and joined to it at the upper floors a second wing that crosses the path on a wide, low segmental
    stone arch: you walk under the building, with a parapet wall and benches beyond. Tall conifers and young trees on
    a lawn in front. (This is the joined-up structure the map has in pieces.)
16. **The three-floor hall from its lawn.** A long, symmetrical limestone building, three floors of evenly spaced
    square windows (four each side of the centre on every floor), a crenellated parapet along the whole roof, and a
    centre block that stands forward and a little taller: a broad round-arched entrance at the ground, a band of small
    windows over it, a wide bay window on the top floor, its own higher crenellated parapet. A small door near the
    right end, a conical-roofed turret behind the right corner, two tall spruces either side of the entrance.

So that building is three floors along its whole length with a larger centre block: the middle stairwell is in it,
with a door on each floor to a TAC officer's family quarters beside the stair. The stairwells at the two ends are
half-turn (switchback) stairs; the middle ones are straight runs. And the halls, the clock tower and the arched
link are to be joined into one structure as in photo 15, from the pieces the map already has.

### The library (the owner, the same day)

"Red coned roof is the library, we already have a red building for it, but it needs improvement to give those
details." So photo 14's low stone building with the tall red conical roof over a round-arched entrance is the
library; the map's red building is to be reworked to show it (stone walls, the cone, the arched entrance), not
replaced with a new place.

### The main entrance (the owner, the same day, with photo 17)

"See that stone obelisk style thing, that is one aspect of it (splits the path/road). You should have another
reference of it directly on." (Photo 9 is that head-on view.)

17. **The entrance, at an angle.** The fieldstone cairn (rounded field stones, a pointed arched niche at its top
    with dressed-stone edges) stands on a low stone-walled flower bed with a concrete kerb, in the middle of the
    drive: the road splits round it. Behind it a two-to-three-storey crenellated limestone building with a square
    corner tower, narrow slit windows in the tower, arched and square windows along the side, an arched doorway at
    the far end and a small arched door in the tower's base; a large tree at the left.

So the main entrance is a drive that splits round the stone cairn in front of the castle-like hall, not a skywalk.

### Another view of "that other building" (the owner, the same day, photo 18)

18. **A three-storey hall at an angle.** (Read here as the three-floor hall of photo 16 from its left end: the owner
    said only "another view of that other building"; confirm before building from it.) Coursed limestone, three
    floors of square windows, a crenellated parapet. The centre block stands forward and taller, with a round turret
    at each of its front corners rising past the parapet, an arched entrance at its foot and a bay of windows
    above. A lower wing at the near end with large round-arched ground-floor openings and a dark double door. A
    small conical-roofed turret at the far end. In front: a flagstone path and a low curved wall of stacked
    limestone with a square pier, lawn, a tall spruce.

### The entrance, from inside it (the owner, the same day, photo 19)

"A view at entrance between those two buildings and the obelisk thing. The gym is to the left (behind) of the
reference image."

19. **Looking in at the entrance.** In the foreground a fieldstone gate pier with a bronze plaque and a round globe
    lamp in an iron cage on top, a low fieldstone wall running from it. Beyond the drive, a three-storey limestone
    keep: two round corner turrets with crenellated tops rising above the roof, red-trimmed square windows, a wide
    arched window on the top floor, a round-arched door at the ground with a wooden notice board under a small shingle
    roof beside it. A red conical roof (the library) shows in the distance at the right. The gym is out of frame,
    to the left and behind the viewer.

So the entrance is: fieldstone gate piers with caged globe lamps and low fieldstone walls at the road, the drive
splitting round the cairn, the two crenellated stone halls either side, the gym off to the left, the library
further in to the right.

### The campus plan (the owner, the same day: a screenshot of the school's own campus map, reference 20)

North is up. Read from the plan (positions are as drawn, not measured):

- **Streets.** Wisconsin Avenue along the top; St. John's Road below the first row of blocks; then Guard Path
  (west) and Church Street (east) on one line; Exeter Street below that. Running north-south: Spanjer Street on the
  west; Smythe Lane and Farrand Lane between the upper blocks; a "Visitor parking" strip down the middle between the
  two central blocks; Genesee Street (Highway C) along the east edge. **The Main Gate is on Genesee Street at the
  east end of St. John's Road**, with the Cadet Post just inside it.
- **North-west central block (between St. John's Road and Guard Path, west of the parking strip):** Noble Victory
  Memorial Chapel in its north-west corner with The Columbarium attached to its south-east; Smythe Hall, an L-shaped
  building, in the east of the block (a short west arm at the top, the long arm running south along the parking
  strip).
- **North-east central block (east of the parking strip, up to Genesee Street):** the Cadet Post at the top by the
  gate; Farrand Hall Gymnasium, a large rectangle across the south of the block; Gerber Pool, a long north-south
  rectangle joined to the gym's east end.
- **South-west central block (between Guard Path and Exeter Street):** Memorial Hall on the west side; DeKoven
  Hall, a long north-south building on the east side by the parking strip, with "Reception" marked at its west
  face; Stangeland / Cord Hall in the south-west; Welles Dining Hall in the south-east, at the foot of DeKoven Hall;
  **the Adreani Memorial Bridge joins Stangeland / Cord Hall to Welles Dining Hall** (this is the skywalk, and it is
  here, not at the main gate). The four buildings ring an open court.
- **South-east central block:** private property, St. John Chrysostom Episcopal Church and cemetery (not part of
  the campus).
- **West of the central blocks, east of Spanjer Street:** the Field of Flags and Parade Field with the Flag Pole at
  its east side; Scott Johnston / Hazelwood Hall along the south of that block, a volleyball court at its west end.
- **South of Exeter Street:** tennis courts (two sets), Mouso Hall, the Carpenter Shop, Birk Infirmary, the Campus
  Store, a US Post Office, maintenance barns.
- **West of Spanjer Street:** the athletics: Price Davis Field at Rick Leone Stadium, the Sturm-Carl Building, Jim
  Duggan Field (baseball), Adreani Track, the JV baseball field, a rappelling tower.

The plan does not name a library; the owner says the red conical roof is the library (ask which block it stands in).

### Scope (the owner, the same day)

"Keep it to the main campus for now." So: the blocks between St. John's Road and Exeter Street, from the Field of
Flags and Scott Johnston / Hazelwood Hall in the west to Genesee Street and the main gate in the east. Out for now:
the athletics west of Spanjer Street (stadium, baseball fields, track, rappelling tower), everything south of Exeter
Street (tennis courts, Mouso Hall, the infirmary, shops, barns), and the blocks north of St. John's Road.

The owner, after: "Leave the houses around St. John's and structures south of Exeter that we have there already."
So what the map already has outside the main campus (the houses round it, the structures south of Exeter Street)
stays exactly as it is: not reworked, not removed. The rework touches the main campus only.

### The chapel again (the owner, the same day: "another view of the chapel", photos 21 and 22)

21. **The nave from the rear gallery.** Looking down the length of the nave to the chancel. Both side walls are
    rough coursed limestone, each with five tall round-arched windows of small-paned stained glass (deep blues with
    flecks of other colours), almost the full height of the wall above a string course. Under each window, at floor
    level, a low segmental-arched opening into a side aisle. A dark timber roof on heavy trusses, flags hanging in two
    rows from it. At the far end a tall round chancel arch, a small round rose window high in the end wall, a white
    reredos and altar under it, steps up to the chancel. Dark wood pews in two blocks with a centre aisle, wall
    sconces between the windows. The view is from a gallery over the entrance.
22. **The entrance front from outside.** A square crenellated tower stands at the front corner, taller than the nave.
    Beside it the gabled end wall with one great round-arched traceried window over a low round-arched porch with red
    double doors and a lantern either side. Down the long side, tall arched traceried windows between stepped
    buttresses, a crenellated parapet above, and a low lean-to aisle with a tiled roof along the foot of the wall
    (the side aisle of photo 21). Low stone terrace walls and steps in front, lawn and large trees.

So the chapel is: a tower at one front corner, a great window over red doors in the end wall, five tall stained
windows a side between buttresses, low side aisles behind arcades, a gallery over the entrance, a timber truss roof,
and a chancel arch with a small rose window beyond.

**How the chapel is laid out inside (the owner, after photos 21 and 22):** "When you first come in, the entrance
had spiral cobble staircases on both sides (left and right) for the upper seating. You enter the main hall from the
right and left beyond and around each side. The shot provided is from the upper seating. The left and right halls
with pillars, those are where you would come in and sit in a pew. Behind the altar in the back is a room and office,
and stairs that lead to a basement area. This is accessible from the back, or from a door at the end of the left
hallway inside of the chapel before the altar area steps."

So, in order from the red doors:
- **Entrance (the narthex):** a spiral stair of cobble stone on the left and another on the right, each up to the
  gallery (the upper seating over the entrance; photo 21 was taken from it).
- **Into the nave:** not straight ahead but round each side: you pass the stairs and come into the left or the right
  side aisle (the "halls with pillars" behind the arcades), and step from an aisle into the pews.
- **Behind the altar:** a room and an office (a sacristy), and a stair down to a basement.
- **Ways to that back part:** a door from outside at the back of the chapel, or a door at the far end of the left
  aisle, just before the steps up to the altar.

For the map this gives the chapel three levels (basement, nave, gallery) and four ways through it (the front doors,
the back door, the two aisles), with the spiral stairs and the basement stair as the vertical routes. A spiral stair
is a new stair piece (the game has straight and half-turn ones): to be specified in the chapel step, wide enough for
bots.

## What it asks of MapMaker (pieces first: no one-off map code)

- A dorm room as a piece: door, sink, desk with shelves, bunk, window, two slim closets either side of the door; and
  a dorm floor laid out as a hall down the middle with rooms both sides. The same hall-and-rooms plan with a classroom
  piece for the school building, on several floors.
- A thick wooden door with iron hardware; better windows (tall sash; arched with stone surrounds for the chapel and
  the stone halls); walls closed where buildings stand open today.
- Current stairs in place of the old custom ones.
- The main entrance without a skywalk (find where the real skywalk is and whether the map has it there).
- The gym as a barrel-vaulted hall with a brick entrance wall; the grounds between library and chapel as lawn, trees,
  paths and the stone monument.

## Before building

Wait for the owner's further references. Then: an inventory of St. John's today (which buildings stand open, where
the old stairs are, what the entrance is), a list of the pieces to add to MapMaker, and the owner's go on the plan.
St. John's is the largest map (about 3,400 entities) and has had performance work (HANDOFF, "St. John's
performance"): measure before and after.
