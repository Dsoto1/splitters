# Tileset 04, Tunnel Systems: the plan (2026-11-13)

The owner's brief is `OWNER_BRIEF_2026-11-13.md`. This is how we build it, in landing steps. Step 1 (the ground) is
built with this plan; the rest is proposed and waits for the owner's answers at the end.

## 1. How it works today, and why it looks wrong

- **Ground.** `map.terrain` is a height grid (`packages/shared/src/map/terrain.ts`): `cols × rows` heights in
  centimetres, 1.25 to 4 m apart, one material and one plant level a square. One height a point: no holes, no
  overhangs. It is drawn square by square (`client/src/mapMeshes.ts` `buildTerrain`), collides as one triangle mesh
  (`terrainTriangles` → Rapier trimesh in `sim/physics.ts` and `sim/effectsPhysics.ts`), and the same triangles go
  into the bots' navmesh (`sim/mapNav.ts`). Nothing else is "the ground": under the surface there is nothing, and a
  body under it is not pushed out.
- **Ground as played.** The file keeps the ground as made. Every load (and MapMaker's view) works out the ground as
  played from it (`map/settle.ts` `withGroundAsPlayed`): paths laid in, then `terrainUnderFloors`: ground within 3 m
  over a floor is pulled just under that floor. Deeper is left alone on purpose (six shipped maps keep rooms and stair
  heads buried deeper).
- **Tunnels.** `core.tunnel_vault` (+ bend, junction, crossing): an 8 m cell, a 3.6 m passage between 2.2 m of
  masonry, a barrel vault to 3.28 m. The `_walled` twins (the Side walls option) add a kerb. Also the Hypogeum set
  pieces (`setpiece.tunnel_*`), `imperial.tunnel`, `frontier.mine_tunnel`, `jungle.cave`, and trenches roofed with
  free blocks (Iron Maw's three tunnels).
- **Why a tunnel under a hill fails.** The hill's surface is above the tunnel in the middle and must be below its
  floor outside the mouth, so somewhere it passes through the passage and closes it. The only tool was to pull the
  ground under the floor all along the tunnel (Flatten, or Cut ground to floors), which removes the hill: a trench
  with a roofed box in it. Sandfall and Iron Maw both record this in their decisions files; Iron Coast gave up on
  tunnels for it.
- **Which map is "woodside"?** No map has that name or id. The nearest are Backwoods Lodge (cellars in a hillside),
  Norman Shores (walkout basements on wooded slopes), Iron Maw (trench tunnels under rock lids) and Whiteout Station
  (its tunnel and ridge). Question 1 below.

## 2. The ground: the design built in step 1

**Chosen: the ground is cut by the tunnel pieces themselves, exactly, and nothing is stored.**

A tunnel piece declares a *bore* in its tile definition (`TileDefinition.bore`, content/tiles/core.json):

- `cut`: boxes in the tile's frame. The part of the ground's surface inside a cut box is removed from the surface:
  from what is drawn, from the collision mesh and from the navmesh input. Ground higher than the box stays (the hill
  on top), ground lower stays, ground outside the footprint is untouched. The removal is exact: the two triangles of
  each grid square are clipped against the box (`map/groundBore.ts` `cutGroundPolygon`), so the ground ends on the
  piece's own outer wall and cap, on any grid spacing, with no gap.
- `open` (+ `out`, the side the mouth faces): a mouth. Over the opening the ground comes down to just under its floor
  (every grid square the opening touches), runs on outward until the ground as made is down to the floor (daylight,
  at most 48 m), and beyond rises no steeper than 1.5 in 1 (56°): a cutting with banks. It only lowers ground.
- A piece with a bore is left out of `terrainUnderFloors`: its floors pull no ground down.

Both are worked out in the ground-as-played step, from the map's tiles, every load and after every edit in MapMaker.
**The map format does not change** (still v36): a map file keeps the hill as the map maker sculpted it, a tunnel that
moves takes its cut and its cutting with it, and Ctrl+Z needs nothing new. The plain and Side-walls vaulted tunnels
have no bore, so every shipped map plays as it did (unit test).

What each system sees:

| System | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| Drawn ground | `buildTerrain` draws a cut square as its remaining pieces; soft paint borders skip cut squares |
| Collision, shots, grenades | `mapTerrainMesh(map)` returns the cut mesh (players, the effects world, the client's prediction all take it from there) |
| Navmesh, bots | Same mesh into Recast: the tunnel floor is walkable under the hill, the hill is walkable above. Bots' coarse moves (big matches, `Match.relaxedMove`) used the height function as the ground and would lift a bot in a tunnel onto the hill: within 2 m of a bore they now make the exact move |
| Vehicles | Suspension and ground rays hit the same mesh. Nothing new; a vehicle fits only a bore wide enough (road family, step 6) |
| Plants, props on the ground | `terrainHeightAt` is the hill. Plants skip ground that is cut away (step 2); props are placed by the map maker |
| Minimap, map plan | Unchanged in step 1: the tunnel's tiles draw as tiles. Step 3 draws buried pieces dashed |
| MapMaker | The view redraws the ground when the cuts change (a tunnel moved under a hill changes no height). The tile inspector's Options gets **Under ground**; the palette gets **Tunnel Portal** |
| validate-map | New warning `tunnel.mouth_buried`: a bored piece's port that leads to nothing built, with earth over its floor just outside |
| Cost | Cuts are cached on the tile list; no per-frame cost. Measured on the coverage map (7 bored tiles, 3 mouths, a 161 × 161 grid): 49 of 25,600 squares clipped, 51,141 triangles instead of 51,200, the collision mesh built in 20 ms, 385 vertices lowered by the three cuttings |

### Earth brought to the pieces (added in step 2)

Step 1's portal stood in a cut hillside: where the hill was low its outer walls showed, and where it was high the
cut could leave the hill's edge hanging over a wing wall. A height grid can't stand a cliff against a wall, so the
piece now says where the earth goes, and the ground as played goes there, **up or down**:

- `rim`: a line with a height at each end. The ground along it goes to that height: a wing wall's sloping top
  (`both`: raised to it where the hill is low, lowered to it where the hill is high), a headwall's coping (`lower`).
  Further out the ground may differ from the rim by 1.5 a metre, so a bank or a berm lies against the wall.
- `cover`: a rectangle the ground is at least so high over: 0.2 m of earth on every cap. A buried tunnel in a dip
  or on flat ground is a mound with the mouths open, never a masonry box. Earth that is brought *up* falls away at
  0.7 in 1 (35°), which players and bots walk, so a mound is crossed over its top; ground that is cut *down* keeps
  the 1.5 in 1 bank.
- A portal's apron is cut out of the ground at any height (so no hill roofs it), and its mouth's flat ground is its
  own width along its outer edge and out to daylight.
- Order where they disagree: the mouth's flat ground (the way in is never banked up), then a rim, then raising over
  lowering. They are bounds on each vertex, so working the ground out twice gives the same ground.

What this means for a map maker: a buried piece raises as well as lowers ground near it (up to its cap, within a few
metres). Question 8.

On a grid that doesn't line up with the 8 m lattice (2.5 m, 1.25 m) the last grid square of a wing wall before the
mouth belongs to the way in, so a little of the wing's foot can show there; on a 4 m grid the banks' toes reach up to
half a metre into the approach in front of the apron. Both are in the unit tests as bounds.

### Alternatives looked at

1. **A hole mask per grid square, stored in the map** (v37, a `holes` array like `foliage`). Simple to draw and
   collide, and Rapier's own heightfield does the same. Rejected: a square is 2 to 4 m and a passage is 3.6 m on an
   8 m lattice that rarely lines up with the grid, so a hole is up to a square wider than the piece on each side: a gap
   you see sky through, which the collar would have to cover at any spacing. It is also hand state: it has to be
   painted, mirrored, resampled by Change grid, migrated, and it goes stale when the tunnel moves.
2. **The same mask, derived from the pieces instead of painted.** Removes the staleness, keeps the gaps.
3. **Exact clipping, derived (chosen).** No gaps, no format change, no new tool to learn. Cost: T-junctions where a
   clipped square meets a whole one (risk 2).
4. **A second height layer / real caves (voxels or a marching mesh).** The only thing that gives free-form caves.
   Far too large for this, and not needed: caves are rock shells inside a bore (section 3).
5. **Does anything below the heightfield need a physics change?** No. The trimesh is a surface, not a solid. A closed
   shell under it already works; the only thing that ever blocked was the surface crossing the passage, which the cut
   removes. Checked by test: walk through, shoot through, straight down from the sky hits the hill.

### What step 1 does not do

- A mouth in flat ground (a tunnel under a field with no hill): the cutting would run 48 m. That is the sloped
  module's job (a ramp or stair down to the bore), step 4.
- Free-angle tunnels: cuts are axis-aligned boxes, like tiles.
- Bores in set pieces work (set pieces are expanded first) but none ships yet.
- The hill on the coverage map is too steep for bots to climb (it was raised with the brush to about 10 m over a
  13 m radius), so "bots walk the hill over the tunnel" is proved in the unit test's ridge, not on that map.

## 3. One structural kit

**Grid.** The Classic Grid as it is: 8 m cells, 4 m levels. A tunnel module is one cell (chambers 2 × 2 and 3 × 3).

**Bore profiles** (the clear opening on a cell edge; every family draws one of these, so any two modules with the
same profile meet without a step):

| Profile | Clear width × height | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S, service | 3.6 × 3.3 m (today's vault: springing 2.2, crown 3.28) | utility corridors, sewers, mine drifts, cave passages |
| L, large | 7.0 × 3.5 m, flat or shallow arch | road tunnel (two lanes for the vehicles we have), main sewer, chamber links |

Two profiles only. A family may line a profile differently (a brick arch, timber sets, a rock shell) but never
narrows it below the profile's collision box, which is the same for every family: movement, shots and bots behave
the same in a concrete tunnel and a cave of the same shape.

**Connection rule.** A port carries a profile (`ports[].bore: 'S' | 'L'`). Two ports connect when their profiles
match; a reducer module (L one side, S the other) joins the two. validate-map warns on a mismatch. Family never
affects whether two ports connect.

**Elevation step.** One level (4 m) per sloped module over one cell (a 1 in 2 ramp or a stair) for S; one level over
two cells (1 in 4) for L, which vehicles climb. A vertical module is one cell, any number of levels (ladder shaft for
S; lift cage or spiral for mines).

**Module shapes** (each for S; those marked L also for L):

| Shape | Ports | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight (L) | 2 opposite | |
| Curve (L) | 2 adjacent | 90° in one cell |
| T-junction (L) | 3 | |
| Four-way (L) | 4 | |
| Dead end | 1 | closed by the family's end wall |
| Slope (L) | 2, one a level up | ramp or stair by family |
| Vertical | 1 at each end level | shaft |
| Chamber 2 × 2, 3 × 3 | on its perimeter | one level high, or two (a cavern) |
| Portal (L) | 1 bore + 1 open | headwall, wings, apron; opens the ground (step 1) |
| Reducer | L + S | |
| Transition | 2 opposite, a family each side | section 4 |

Every module has a bore `cut` (its cells up to its cap), so ground-on-top is a property of the kit, not an option.

**One geometry, five families.** A module is generated, not hand-written: `tools/content/kit/tunnel.mjs` (new) makes
each shape's collision boxes once (the shared shell: floor, side masses, cap) and, per family, the lining that is
drawn inside it. Two ways to carry the family on a placed piece:

- (a) a tile id per shape and family (`tunnel.straight.concrete` …) with the inspector switching between them, the
  way Side walls does. No format change; 11 shapes × 5 families is 55 tiles before L and transitions.
- (b) **recommended:** one tile id per shape, and `TilePlacement.family` (map format v37; optional, absent = the
  shape's first family; migration is a no-op, as v31→v32 was; `migrate.ts`, `schema.ts` and their tests, per the
  earlier bumps). The inspector shows a **Family** select on tunnel pieces, multi-select changes a whole run at once,
  and the palette shows a shape once with a family switch at the top of the Tunnels group. Collision never reads the
  family.

**Caves are shells, not squares.** The cave family's lining is a rock shell: irregular rock (seeded noise on a
lofted ring) drawn inside the profile's collision box, with the floor uneven by less than a step (collision stays
the flat floor plus a few low boxes). Chambers take stalactite and stalagmite kit props and a collapsed-opening
variant of the portal (boulders for wings, no headwall). Where a cave module meets any masonry family the
**collar** is the transition piece: a ring of that family's masonry with broken rock overlapping it.

**Transitions.** One cell, a family each side, the joint in the middle: concrete ↔ cave (cast collar, rock
breaking over it), brick ↔ concrete (old arch into a cast frame with a grate), mine ↔ road (timber sets into a
lined bay with a roll-up door), mine ↔ cave, brick ↔ cave. Generated from the two families' linings plus a joint
part, so any pair works; the five named pairs get a designed joint.

## Step 2 as built

- **Data:** `content/tiles/tunnels.json`: `profiles` (S: 3.6 m wide, 3.3 m clear, cap at 3.75 m), `families`
  (concrete: its materials, lining and trim) and `shapes` (the sides each passage runs to; the portal's headwall and
  wings). `tools/content/kit/tunnel.mjs` makes a kit tile for each family and shape, and `tools/content/tiles.mjs`
  checks and writes them with the rest of the kit. A second family is a second entry under `families`: no geometry
  code.
- **Ids:** `core.tunnel_<family>_<shape>` (`core.tunnel_concrete_straight`, `_curve`, `_t`, `_cross`, `_end`,
  `_portal`). No map format change. If the owner picks the `family` field (question 2), these six become the
  shapes' first family and the ids stay valid.
- **What is shared:** the collision of a shape is the shell only: the floor, the masses either side of the passage,
  the cap, and a portal's headwall and wing walls (at most 9 colliding boxes a module, 20 for the portal). A unit
  test fires rays down every port of every shape: the passage is the same clear box, 3.6 m by 3.3 m, in all of
  them. Everything a family adds is `trim` (drawn, never collided with).
- **Concrete family:** `core.pbr_concrete` shell, soffit and floor, `core.pbr_concrete_dark` plinth, joint ribs and
  coping; a cast downstand where wall meets soffit; a panel joint every 2 m; a tread-plate drain down the floor; a
  rusted pipe run on the west wall of straight runs (straight, portal); a steel service door on the dead end; a
  hazard-stripe band (`core.hazard`) on each jamb of the portal and on the door.
- **Lights:** one strip-light fixture at the crown of every module: a housing and a tube in the glowing library
  material (`core.lamp_glow`, warm white). It is a glowing surface, not a light entity: **0 real lights a module**,
  nothing against the lights budget, steady (no flicker to calm). A map maker who wants pools of light adds light
  entities; a set piece with them is step 3.
- **The mouth:** both portals (vaulted and concrete) have wing walls under a sloping coping (one tilted box each,
  stepped masonry hidden under it) from 3.75 m at the headwall to 1.3 m at an end pier, and the rims and cover
  above. Two portals side by side share a wall between their aprons and read as two mouths.
- **Joining the old tunnels:** the vaulted tunnels have the same 3.6 m passage, so a concrete piece meets a vaulted
  one with no step (the coverage map has them joined in three places).

### Checked in step 2 (the three things step 1 left unverified)

- **Plants in cut ground.** They did grow there: on a portal's apron, on its wing walls and in the passage's first
  metres (the plants stand at the ground's height, and the ground is still "there" where it is cut). Fixed: the
  ground as played remembers its cuts (`terrainCuts`), and plants skip ground that is cut away. Unit test
  (`client/test/foliage.test.ts`) and a look at an overgrown scratch copy of the coverage map.
- **Seams between a clipped and a whole grid square.** Every point of a clipped square that lies on its border is on
  the ground's own surface there to 1e-14 m (measured on the coverage map: 16 clipped squares, 40 border points), so
  the edges coincide and only rasterising could open a hairline. None seen in the pictures (looking up from the
  aprons at the cut edges against the sky). Not hunted at every angle and distance.
- **A bore next to water.** A pond dug beside the mound, its surface under the tunnel's floor: fine. Water whose box
  crosses a passage above its floor floods it: it is drawn in the passage and you wade in it. Liquids are boxes and
  know nothing of walls; that is true of any room today, not new with tunnels. A lake over a tunnel therefore needs
  its water box kept above the cap. A warning for a liquid overlapping a bore is cheap to add (step 3).

## Step 3 as built (the owner's answers, 2026-11-13)

- **Family is a setting on the placed piece: map format v37.** `TilePlacement.family` (optional; absent = the
  tile's own). A v36 map opens unchanged (a no-op migration, as v35→v36 was; `tunnelFamily.test.ts`). A shape's
  tiles in every family share cells, ports, collision and bore; only `catalog.drawn(placement)` (what
  `buildMapGeometry` and the decor draw) reads the family. The six `core.tunnel_concrete_*` ids are the shapes; a
  family's own id (`core.tunnel_brick_cross`) is valid too and hidden from the palette. A family the tile doesn't
  come in is ignored, with a validator warning (`tile.family_unknown`).
- **A second family to switch to: brick**, as data only (old brick, flagstone floor, a cobble drain, a lamp). It is a
  stand-in so the switch is real: its arched lining, water channel, gates and lanterns are step 5.
- **MapMaker, the tile inspector's Tunnel box:** *Family* (a select; with *Whole run* ticked it changes every buried
  piece joined to the selection, port to port, through set pieces' tunnels too, in one undo step) and *Add a portal at
  the open end* (a portal on each end of the selected pieces that leads to nothing built, turned to face out, in the
  piece's family; the vaulted tunnels get the vaulted portal).
- **Buried passages dashed** on the menu's map plan, the minimap and, in MapMaker, on the ground over them (drawn
  over everything, so a run under a hill can be followed from above). Buried pieces are no longer drawn as floors
  and walls on the plan and minimap.
- **Lit Tunnel Run** (`content/setpieces/tunnels.json`): three concrete modules with a real light at each fixture
  (3 lights, warm, 9 m range, against the map's lights). Single modules stay glow only.
- **Liquid in a bore** is a validator warning (`tunnel.flooded`).
- **Flat ground: "flush with other terrain if able".** What it turned out to mean:
  - A piece's cover is 0.1 m of earth over its cap. New terrain lies 0.15 m under a level and a cap is 0.25 m under
    it, so **a run placed a level down under flat ground adds nothing: the surface stays exactly as made** (unit
    test: every height unchanged over and beside the run). Things standing on that ground stay where they are.
  - **The mouth gets a way in**: level with the floor for 2 m, then an earth ramp at 0.35 in 1 (19°) up to the
    ground as made, with banks. For a mouth 4 m down that is a cutting about 14 m long and, at the mouth, 8 m of
    cell plus a few metres of bank wide; the headwall's top stands 0.9 m over the field as a parapet. This also
    replaces step 1's level cutting in a hillside (the ramp only shortens it) and its 48 m limit rarely matters now.
  - **What still mounds:** a buried piece on level 0 of flat ground (its cap is 3.75 m over the field: a 3.85 m
    mound), and any piece whose cap is above the ground as made by more than 0.1 m (a run crossing a dip, the low
    flanks of a hill). The mound is exactly the missing earth, no more. A map maker who wants no mound places the
    run a level down, or raises the hill.
  - Not "able": a run a level down under ground that is itself lower than the level (a hollow) still gets earth
    over the cap there.
- **Finish faults:** the pipe is eight-sided (a second bar turned 45°; twelve-sided and thicker since step 4). A prop bedded on ground a tunnel cut away
  stands on the floor under it as played. **Not done:** the two portals' colour mismatch (the vaulted portal takes
  the map theme's paint, the kit's portal its family's materials; they are different families by design: use one
  kind at a twin mouth, or paint the vaulted one in the inspector).

## Step 4 as built

Six more shapes in `content/tiles/tunnels.json`, so every family has them (`kind` says a piece is more than one cell
or level; `tools/content/kit/tunnel.mjs` `specialParts`):

| Shape | Cells | Ports | Ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stairs, Slope (`flight`) | 1, two levels | south at its level, north a level up | cut and cover in four 2 m stretches that climb with the soffit: between two buried levels nothing is raised; up to level 0 the top is under a 3.85 m mound |
| Surface Stairs (`descent`) | 1, two levels | south (the tunnel), north a level up (the field) | cut open to the sky over the stairwell; rims bring the field level to the walls and the top step; no cover |
| Shaft (`hatch` on a straight) | 1 | north, south | a second cut up through the cap (the hatch), rims level to it |
| Riser | 1, two levels | south, north a level up | one cut, cover over its top (flush two levels down) |
| Chamber | 2 x 2 | S and W of cell (0,0), N of (0,1), E of (1,0) | one cut and cover, as a module |

- **Same passage, same levels.** Every port is the S profile (3.6 x 3.3 m) on a cell edge; a level is 4 m. The
  stairs are 16 steps of 0.25 m (as `core.stairs`); the slope is 1 in 2 (26.6 degrees, walked; too steep for wheels:
  the road family's ramp is 1 in 4 over two cells, step 6).
- **Ladders are entities**, so a shaft or riser tile alone has none: the set pieces Tunnel Shaft and Tunnel Riser
  carry them (and are fixed to the concrete family until set pieces take a family, step 5).
- **Bots.** Stairs, slope and chamber are plain navmesh. A ladder is a pair of links (foot to top, top to foot), as
  on every shipped map; bots climb both ways. Tested: in a sealed tunnel whose only way out is the shaft's ladder a
  bot is on the field 13 s later, having met the other bot coming down in the hatch. What they do not do: queue (two
  bots at one ladder push past each other, which costs seconds), fight on a ladder, or jump down a shaft as a short
  cut (they take the ladder down; a player can drop the 3.85 m).
- **A shaft's top**: open is the Shaft (hatch flush with flat ground, 0.2 m kerb, lid as a marker); capped is the
  Riser, or simply no shaft. A deep open shaft (two levels to the surface) is not a piece: a Riser under a Shaft's
  run does it in two ladders.
- **Plans** draw a piece of more than one cell by its own sides (`bore.plan`); steps open to the sky are not an
  open end for Add a portal (`bore.sky`).
- **MapMaker.** Select picks a buried piece through the ground (level 0, click over a tunnel a level down); Erase
  does not. A selected set piece's buried parts get Add a portal.
- **Not done.** An unused chamber opening is not closed by itself (Sides, or a Dead End). The chamber has no real
  lights (glowing fixtures only; a lit chamber set piece is cheap to add). Surface stairs come with steps only (no
  ramp variant). The vaulted family has none of these shapes (it is the older, theme-painted kit).

### Backwoods Lodge ("woodside"): what its rebuild needs

Its tunnel is "the long drain from the creek that comes up in the cellar": 12 level-0 corridor tiles (9 straights,
a corner, a T, a dead end) from the creek at about (-24, -40) to the lodge, ending in a `core.stairs` up into the
cellar; the "hill" over it is 34 free blocks painted grass (an 8 × 0.4 × 8 m lid over each cell and 4.5 m blocks
either side). That is the box in a trench the owner means.

- Have now: straight, curve, T, dead end, portal; the cover (the drain becomes an earth mound with no blocks, or
  the map maker raises the hill over it); the mouth at the creek.
- Needs step 4: the **slope/stair module**, to come up a level into the cellar as a buried piece (today's
  `core.stairs` would join on, but it is an ordinary tile: the ground is pulled to its floor within 3 m).
- Needs step 5: the **brick sewer family proper** (arched lining, water channel) and its **round barred outfall**
  for the creek end.
- To check at the rebuild: the creek. Its water must stay out of the bore (the new `tunnel.flooded` warning says
  if it doesn't), and the mouth's ramp will cut into the creek bank.
- Nothing in steps 3 to 5 is missing for it beyond those.

## 7. Risks

1. **The portal is masonry on an earth slope, not a sculpted fit.** The cutting's banks follow the grid (2 to 4 m),
   so the wing walls sometimes stand a little proud of the bank, sometimes hold it. Good from play distance; a close
   look shows it. A finer fit would need the banks drawn by the piece (a skirt mesh), which step 2 can add to the
   portal generator if the owner wants it.
2. **T-junctions** where a clipped square meets a whole one: a hairline of sky can flicker at some angles, and
   Rapier can catch an edge. Not seen in the tests; if it shows, the fix is to split the neighbour's edge too.
3. **Banks at 56°** are meant to be unwalkable. A hill made steeper than that by hand next to a mouth is left as it
   is (we only lower).
4. **Scattered props and environment species** placed by the ground's height in cut ground are not filtered (plants
   are, since step 2). A map maker's own prop on an apron stands on the earth's height there, not the floor.
5. **Roads and tracks** laid over a tunnel mouth take their height from the hill, not the tunnel floor. Step 6.
6. **A bore under water or under another tile's sunken floor** is untested.
7. **Coverage of old maps:** nothing changes for them by design; the all-maps tests are the guard.

## 8. Questions for the owner

1. **Which map is "woodside"?** Recommendation: tell us the map; until then we treat Iron Maw (real tunnels through
   rock, today trenches under lids) as the first rebuild, since it shows the problem most.
2. **Family on a placed piece: a tile per family, or a `family` field (map format v37)?** Recommendation: the field.
   One shape in the palette, one switch, whole runs changed at once, and 5× fewer tile ids.
3. **Two bore sizes (3.6 m and 7 m) or more?** Recommendation: two. Every extra size multiplies every shape and
   every transition.
4. **Should the old vaulted tunnels keep their Under ground option once the kit exists?** Recommendation: yes, it
   is cheap and Sandfall-style maps use them; the new kit is where new work goes.
5. **Do you want the banks at a mouth shaped by the portal piece (a skirt of earth and rock that always fits), or
   is the cut hillside enough?** Recommendation: look at the step-1 pictures first; add the skirt in step 2 only if
   the mouth reads as too raw.
6. **Tunnel mouths in flat ground** (stairs or a ramp down): step 4 as planned, or sooner? Recommendation: step 4,
   after the first family, because it needs the slope module anyway.
7. **Order of families after concrete.** Recommendation: sewer and mine together (step 5), road (step 6), caves
   last (step 7) because the rock shell is the most new geometry.
8. **A buried piece now raises ground as well as lowering it** (earth over its cap and up its outer walls, within
   a few metres of it): on flat ground a buried run is a mound. Is that what "under ground" should mean, or should
   a buried piece only ever lower ground and show masonry where the hill doesn't reach? Recommendation: keep the
   mound; it is what makes the mouth look finished on any slope, and a map maker who wants bare masonry uses the
   plain or Side walls tunnels.
