# Audio

**Status:** first audio pass (October 2026), then the Sonniss pass (2026-11-22). Every shipped sound is **CC0** (Kenney, and a few Freesound recordings by named authors), **synthesised by our own code**, or **recorded, from the Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundles** (royalty free, no credit needed; [below](#recorded-sounds-from-the-sonniss-bundles)). The one exception is **the owner's own music and announcer** (2026-11-18): tracks and lines the owner made themself through Artlist, which the owner cleared for the game (owner, 2026-11-17: shipped inside the game only, never as separate downloads; [`AUDIO_MUSIC_ANNOUNCER_PLAN.md`](AUDIO_MUSIC_ANNOUNCER_PLAN.md)). Artlist's stock catalog stays excluded; see [`ASSET_SOURCES.md`](ASSET_SOURCES.md).

The synthesiser is ordinary signal processing written by us, in the same spirit as the procedural map materials. Each weapon is a recipe of layers (supersonic crack, filtered noise body, low "thump" sweep, room tail, mechanical clicks, echoes), rendered at load time with fixed seeds, so it always sounds the same. It is a stand-in for recorded gunshots: when we get CC0 recordings (Sonniss GDC bundles, Freesound CC0, our own foley), switch that sound's entry in `content/sounds.json` from `synth` to file variants. No code changes.

The cloud development environment could not reach Freesound, OpenGameArt, itch.io creator pages or Sonniss when the first pass was made; the owner put the Sonniss bundles in their Drive instead (below).

## Recorded sounds from the Sonniss bundles

The owner's Drive `sounds/sfx/` holds a selection from the Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundles (folders `GDC 2015` to `GDC 2019` and `GDC 2020-2026/{2020,2026}`, about 640 files; approved 2026-11-06, CLAUDE.md "Content rules"). They are royalty free and need no credit; the bundles' files themselves may never be passed on, so none enter the repo.

- **Picking (2026-11-22):** for each sound, the best fit on what it is (a close, clean, off-axis mic for gunshots; on-gun and far mics rejected), length and how it sits beside the sound it replaces, plus two alternates. Weak placeholders were replaced first (the synthesised everyday guns, the generic Kenney impacts and footsteps); the sci-fi guns, hit markers, kill confirm, Freesound flesh hits, UI chimes, pickups and the owner's announcer and music were kept.
- **Processing:** `node tools/content/sonniss-audio.mjs <folder holding the Drive's sfx folder>` reads each variant of `content/sounds.json` whose pack is `processed` (`sonniss-gdc-<year>`): `file` is the original under the pack's `drive` folder, `start` and `length` the part kept (one shot: the last of a burst, so its tail rings out; the next shot's start for single shots). It cuts that part, folds it to mono (`stereo` for the weather beds), compresses it when `punch` is set (a recording too thin beside the synthesised gun it replaces), fades it (3 ms in, `fade` out; `window` for the weather beds: a sine-squared fade over each half, so the copies laid every 1.2 s stay steady), sets its peak to `processing.peakDb` (-1 dBFS, plus the variant's `level`) and encodes Ogg Opus (48 kbps mono, 64 kbps stereo) to `packages/client/public/sounds/<id>_<n>.ogg`. `content/sonniss.lock.json` records each file with a hash of its cut; `pnpm content:check` holds the shipped files to the lock and the manifest (a changed cut that was never rebuilt fails) and to the 128 KB budget, and `pnpm content:build` writes provenance (the studio as creator, the bundle as title, licence `LicenseRef-Sonniss-GDC`, its note in `content/licenses/sonniss-gdc.txt`). `ONLY=<id,id>` rebuilds just those sounds.
- **Levels:** each pick's `gain` was set so its loudest 50 ms match the sound it replaced at that sound's gain (capped at 1.3; the recorded guns sit a little under the synthesiser's, which was dense). Ranges, jitter and voice caps are unchanged.
- **Guns louder, more compressed (owner 2026-11-22, "2b"):** every recorded gun and its alternates is cut with `punch: "gun"` (`GUN_PUNCH` in the tool: the cut brought to a 0 dBFS peak, a 3:1 compressor at -20 dB with a 1 ms attack so the crack's front gets through, +9.5 dB makeup, then a look-ahead limiter 6 dB into a 0 dBFS ceiling), its peak set to -1 dBFS as for every sound, and its decoded Opus peak held under `processing.gunCeilingDb` (-0.5 dBFS; Opus overshoots a limited crack by up to 2 dB, so the tool lowers the level until it doesn't). Gains were then set to these in-game levels (the loudest 50 ms, dBFS, file times gain): pistol -15.3 → -10.5, rifle -13.1 → -9.5, SMG -15.2 → -11.0, shotgun -11.1 → -7.1, sniper -7.4 → -5.9, heavy -9.4 → -8.3 (at the 1.3 cap), revolver -6.4 → -5.8; each gun's alternates to the same level where the cap allows. For comparison: explosion -5.0, the sci-fi guns -14.7 (laser) to -4.5 (vortex; plasma -10.7, railgun -8.4, launcher -5.6), the announcer's lines about -12 on their own bus. Every gun stays under the explosion, and the master limiter is unchanged.
- **The shotgun pump (owner, "3a"):** a weapon's `view.cycle` (`{ sound, at }`, content/weapons) is a pump or bolt worked after each shot, `at` seconds after it, for you (after the shot's own delay) and from other players' guns. The pump shotgun's is `fire.shotgun.pump` (TS Sound: Shotgun_Quick Pump_01, the first pump), 0.3 s after the shot, so it ends by the next shot (45 ticks, 0.75 s); there is no pump animation to follow. The auto, revolving and sawn-off shotguns share the report but aren't pump guns. `tools/content/weapons.mjs` checks the sound exists and that the pump starts inside the fire interval.
- **Footsteps by surface (owner, "4a"):** the step under your feet, and under other players', is looked up on your machine from the map (a short ray down the effects world: a tile face's painted material or its theme's, a free block's material, a prop's or plant's surface, the terrain cell's ground; `View.surfaceUnder`, `fxMap.ts surfaceOfFace`), so nothing is sent. The surface type's footstep family (content/surface-types.json) picks the sound: `footstep` (concrete, stone, brick, tiles, glass, plastic: the Tovusound asphalt boots), `footstep.metal` (Tovusound metal boots, 4), `footstep.wood` (Levan Nadashvili wood, 2: all the bundles have), `footstep.grass` (Tovusound grass sneakers, 4; foliage too), `footstep.dirt` (Levan Nadashvili ground and PMSFX dirt singles, 4; sand and fabric too), `footstep.gravel` (PMSFX dirt and gravel walk, 4), `footstep.snow` (Bluezone snow, 4), each levelled to the plain footstep's in-game level (metal 1 dB over). Every library material names its surface (`tools/content/material-surfaces.mjs`, below). Bullet impacts on tiles now use the same face lookup, so a round into a plank floor sounds and looks like wood.
- **Alternates for the Weapon Manager:** the guns' and reloads' alternates ship as their own sounds (`fire.rifle.b`, `fire.rifle.c`, ...; about 5 KB each), so the Manager's sound picker offers them under "Weapon fire" and "Reload and handling". The rest are listed below only (the impacts on stone, metal and wood carry their first alternate as a second variant).
- **Size:** `public/sounds` went from 932 KB (79 files) to 778 KB (102 files): Opus mono is a fraction of the Kenney packs' stereo Vorbis.
- **Listening:** the helper's audition page (each sound now, the pick and the alternates) was made for the owner; it is not shipped.

## How it fits together

- **`content/sounds.json`** is the single list of game sounds. Each sound has variants (files or a synth recipe) and mix settings: `gain`, `range` (`near` / `mid` / `far`: how far it carries), `jitter` (random pitch spread so repeats don't sound identical) and `rate`.
- **`pnpm content:build`** copies the selected Ogg files from `assets-src/kenney-*` to `packages/client/public/sounds/<id>_<n>.ogg`, removes stale files and adds each file to `content/provenance.json`. **`pnpm content:check`** (CI) verifies they're current, under budget (128 KB each) and CC0/CC-BY.
- **`AudioSystem`** (`packages/client/src/audio/audio.ts`) is one Web Audio graph for the whole app: per-voice gain → distance low-pass → HRTF panner → master volume → limiter. It caps voices (6 per sound, 40 total) and culls sounds beyond their range. Your own sounds (unpositioned) are only ever cut by your own; a world sound over a cap replaces the farthest world voice and is dropped when it is the farthest itself, and a cut voice fades over 15 ms (owner, 2026-11-18: in a 64-player Deathmatch about 110 bot rifle shots a second are heard, and the old oldest-first cut ended your own rifle's 0.4 s report after about 50 ms, every shot; `voiceToSteal`, `test/shotAudio.test.ts`). Your shots that one slow frame runs together keep their ticks' spacing (`shotDelay`, at most 50 ms, nothing at 60 frames a second). The browser only allows audio after a user gesture, so the first click or key press starts it.
- **`SessionAudio`** (`packages/client/src/game/sessionAudio.ts`) maps match events to sounds:
  - Your own shots, footsteps, landings, reloads, weapon switches and dry fire play instantly from client prediction and aren't positioned.
  - Other players' shots, impacts (at most two per shotgun blast), knife swings, deaths, pickups, footsteps and explosions are positioned in the world.
  - The hit marker and kill confirm play when the server confirms a hit or kill. Two kills within 4 s trigger "Multi kill". Round start and end have announcer lines.
- **Volume** is a slider in the pause menu, saved in the browser. **Music** and **Announcer** have their own sliders under it in Options (the Announcer in the pause menu too), saved as `cs.music` (default 50 %) and `cs.announcer` (100 %); 0 is off.
- **The owner's music and announcer** (`content/owner-audio.json`): `node tools/content/owner-audio.mjs <folder with the owner's files>` trims their silence, evens loudness to -16 LUFS (two-pass EBU R128) and encodes Ogg Opus (music 64 kbps stereo to `public/music/`, lines 48 kbps mono to `public/voice/`), recording each in `content/owner-audio.lock.json`; `pnpm content:build` puts them in provenance (owned) and `pnpm content:check` holds the files to the lock and the budgets (2 MB a track, 14 MB of music, 64 KB a line). The owner's originals never go in the repo, and the files are never listed or offered for download on their own.
- **Music** (`audio/music.ts`): each scene's tracks shuffled (none again until all have played), 3 s crossfades, a 1.2 s fade out when a match starts; streamed through an `<audio>` element into the music's bus, so only the playing track is fetched, and only after the first click or key (the menu's first paint and a match's start never wait on it); a hidden tab pauses it where it is. Browser tests leave it off unless `?music=1` (`?music=0` turns it off anywhere).
- **Announcer** (`audio/announcer.ts`): one line at a time, at least 2.5 s from one start to the next, the most important first (match flow, objectives, multi-kills, streaks, special kills, hype); a newer line of the same kind replaces a waiting one, and a line that waited too long is dropped. Its lines load after the game's sounds.

## Tests

- `packages/client/test/audio.test.ts`: every recipe renders finite, normalised, non-silent and deterministic samples that end in silence; the manifest only names known recipes and built files; every weapon has a fire sound and every item kind a pickup sound.
- e2e (`pnpm e2e`): all sounds load and decode in Chromium, and an offline bot match plays sounds.

## Known gaps

- **The sci-fi guns and launchers are synthesised** (the everyday guns are recorded since the Sonniss pass).
- **Ogg Vorbis** files may not decode on older Safari versions. Those sounds are skipped and the game keeps running. Add an AAC/MP3 copy in the build if Safari matters before then.
- No occlusion (sounds pass through walls), reverb zones, ambience beds or grenade bounces yet. Footsteps on roads (paths) follow the terrain cell under them, not the road's look. Remote reloads aren't audible because snapshots don't carry them.
